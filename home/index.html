Off the clock

The week that is not only git history.

Away from production APIs and kernel contracts, I cook, play games, and keep up with people online. None of that is a personal-brand strategy — it is just how I spend a week that is not only git history.

I am a foodie, and I rotate through three kitchens: Cajun, Italian, and Japanese. One night that means real heat in a pot, the next a sauce that takes patience, then something precise with rice and a pan. If I am not shipping code, there is a decent chance I am grocery-listing the next one of those.

Board games take the rest of the table. Euro games when I want an engine to build, card games when the conversation should stay in the round, and press-your-luck titles when I want that extra draw I probably should not take. KeyForge is a regular favorite: every deck is unique, and I like sitting down knowing nobody else is holding the same cards. I was at Gen Con this year in Indianapolis — the closest thing tabletop has to a home game when you live in Indiana.

I also spend a healthy amount of time on social media. It is how I stay in the mix with friends, food, and the hobby. That same community streak shows up in the work: years tutoring STEM at Indiana University, then building Homework Central for the people who used to live in a Discord.
